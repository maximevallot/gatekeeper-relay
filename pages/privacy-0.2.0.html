Gatekeeper privacy policy

Last updated 18 October 2026 (the chat now also knows whether a calendar is connected; before that, 17 October: added exam mode, your local time and calendar deadlines to what the chat, the gate and loosening send, your talk with Gatekeeper kept in your browser, and the time check). Applies to the Gatekeeper browser extension.

Gatekeeper is made by Maxime Vallot, an individual developer. Contact: mvallot22@gmail.com.

The short version

  • Your rules, your budgets and the time you spend on sites stay in your browser. Two exceptions: when you use the talking gate, the message sends the few facts it needs to answer; and when you change your rules by chat, your rules are sent so they can be changed. Both are listed below. The time you spend on sites is never sent by the chat.
  • Gatekeeper never reads what is on a web page. It has no code that runs inside web pages.
  • Apart from a time check that carries nothing, four features send a little data to our server, and all are off until you turn them on: the gate that can talk back, sorting sites Gatekeeper doesn't know, changing your rules by chat, and judging YouTube videos for your video rules. Gatekeeper asks you before the first request of each.
  • We do not sell your data, use it for advertising, or build a profile of you.

What stays in your browser

These are stored by your browser on your computer, in the extension's own storage. Removing the extension deletes them. None of it is sent anywhere, except the items listed in the next section, and only when you use those features.

  • Your rules: which kinds of site you limit, the minutes for each, your own sites and any limit you gave a single site, your video rules, and the goal sentence you wrote.
  • Exam mode, while it is on: when it ends and what it is for.
  • Your talk with Gatekeeper: the last 50 messages of the chat where you change your rules, and the change waiting for you to press Apply. The toolbar, the Talk page and the rules page all show this one thread. "Forget this talk" deletes it.
  • If you paste a calendar link (for example Canvas's calendar feed): the link, and the events in the next 48 hours read from it. Gatekeeper reads the calendar from your computer; the calendar's own service (Canvas, Google) sees that request, as it would from your calendar app.
  • Your usage: minutes spent on each kind of site today, passes the gate granted, and rule changes waiting for the next morning.
  • The kind Gatekeeper decided each unknown site belongs to, so it does not ask again.
  • Which of your video rules each YouTube video you opened falls under, kept by video ID for the 300 most recent videos, so each is judged once.
  • A random install number made when you install Gatekeeper.

To work, Gatekeeper checks the address of the tab in front of you, and of any tab playing sound, every few seconds, on your computer, only to match it to a kind of site and count time. It also checks which sites your browser lets it see, so a site you take away from it stays closed.

If you have a video rule, Gatekeeper asks YouTube, through YouTube's public oEmbed address, for the title and channel of each YouTube video you open, using the video's ID. YouTube already knows you are watching that video; nothing else is sent to YouTube. Without your yes to judging by Claude, the title and channel are compared with your video rules' words on your computer and go nowhere else.

Gatekeeper for Mac, if you connect it

If you install Gatekeeper for Mac and turn on "Mac app" in the extension's settings (your browser asks first), the extension shares with it, on your computer: your goal sentence, each watched kind's minutes for today and whether it is open or closed, and exam mode with its end time and goal. The Mac app keeps the latest copy in a file in your user folder (~/Library/Application Support/Gatekeeper/today.json), where other apps you run can read it. It can ask the extension to start exam mode, never to loosen anything. None of this goes to our server or to anyone else. Turn "Mac app" off, or remove either app, to stop it.

What is sent to our server, and only if you turn it on

Our server is a small relay that passes a request to an AI model and returns the answer.

One request goes to it whatever you turn on: when your computer's clock jumps (after sleep, or if it's changed), Gatekeeper asks its relay for the time, so a changed clock can't end a limit early. Nothing is sent with that request, not even your install number.

Never sent: full page addresses, page content, your browsing history, anything from other tabs, your rules other than to the rules chat, your calendar link, or calendar events other than the deadlines sent with a gate message.

You can turn any of these off at any time on the extension's rules page. Nothing is sent after that, and Gatekeeper keeps working with its built-in list of sites; your video rules then match their words on your computer. Turning off judging by Claude also forgets which rule Claude said each video falls under.

Who receives it

  • Cloudflare runs our relay. Our relay does not log what you send. Cloudflare keeps its usual technical records of requests, such as time and network address, under its own terms.
  • Anthropic provides the AI model (Claude). The relay sends it the data listed above so it can answer. Under Anthropic's commercial terms, API requests are not used to train its models, and Anthropic keeps them for a limited time under its own terms.
  • Nobody else. No advertisers, analytics companies or data brokers.

What we keep on our side

  • For each install number and each network address: a count of AI requests and tokens per day, to cap use and prevent abuse. These counts are not linked to anything else about you.
  • For each domain: the kind it was sorted into (for example agar.io is a game), for up to 90 days, shared by everyone and not linked to who asked.

We do not store the domains you visit against your install number, page titles, video titles or channels, your rules, your goal sentence, exam mode, your calendar deadlines, or what you type at the gate or in the rules chat. Video judgments are not cached on our side.

What we never do

  • Sell your data or share it for advertising.
  • Use it for anything other than limiting the time you spend on distracting sites.
  • Let a person read it, unless you ask us to look into a problem, the law requires it, or we must investigate abuse.

Your choices

  • Everything Gatekeeper keeps about you is in your browser. Change it on the rules page; remove the extension to delete it.
  • To have the daily counts for your install number deleted, write to mvallot22@gmail.com.

Security

Everything sent to our relay travels over HTTPS. The key to the AI model is held by the relay and never ships inside the extension.

Children

Gatekeeper is not meant for children under 13, and we do not knowingly collect their data.

Changes

If we change what Gatekeeper sends, the extension will say so and ask again before the change takes effect, and this page will show a new date.

Limited Use

Gatekeeper's use of information received from your browser adheres to the Chrome Web Store User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements.
